Lecture 18: Concavity and The 2nd Derivative Test

Goal: Use the 2nd derivative to determine properties of the original function.

Concave Up [CU]

\( f' \) is increasing

\( [f']' = f'' > 0 \)

Concave Down [CD]

\( f' \) is decreasing

\( \Rightarrow [f']' = f'' < 0 \)

Theorem (C Test)

Let \( f \) be a function where \( f'' \) exists on an open interval \( I \)

  1. If \( f''(x) > 0 \) for \( x \) in \( I \), \( f \) is CU on \( I \).
  2. If \( f''(x) < 0 \) for \( x \) in \( I \), \( f \) is CD on \( I \).

Example 1

Discuss the shape of \( f(x) = x^3 - 12x + 1 \)

Step 1 Find when \( f' = 0 \) and \( f'' = 0 \).

\( f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12 = 3(x-2)(x+2) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \)

\( \Rightarrow x = \pm 2 \)

\( f''(x) = 6x \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \)

\( \Rightarrow x = 0 \)

Step 2 Make sign chart for both \( f' \) and \( f'' \)

Conclusion:

\( f \) is increasing and CD on \( (-\infty, -2) \)

\( f \) is decreasing and CD on \( (-2, 0) \)

\( f \) is decreasing and CU on \( (0, 2) \)

\( f \) is increasing and CU on \( (2, \infty) \)

Def If \( f \) changes concavity at a point \( P \), then \( P \) is called an inflection point (IP).

Theorem If \( (c, f(c)) \) is an IP, either \( f''(c) = 0 \) or undefined.

Why? \( c \) is a critical number of \( f' \)

NOTE:

The reverse is not true

\( \Rightarrow x = -\frac{1}{3}, 1 \) are the only possible locations.

Concavity can be used to detect relative extrema. Let \( c \) be a critical number of \( f \)

\( f''(c) > 0 \Rightarrow f \) is CU near \( c \)

\( f''(c) < 0 \Rightarrow f \) is CD near \( c \)

Theorem (2nd Derivative Test)

Let \( f'' \) be continuous at \( c \) and \( f'(c) = 0 \).

  1. If \( f''(c) > 0 \), then \( f \) has a rel. min. at \( c \)
  2. If \( f''(c) < 0 \), then \( f \) has a rel. max at \( c \)
  3. If \( f''(c) = 0 \), the test is inconclusive. [Ie, pick another test]

Why (3)?

Example 4

Locate and classify all relative extrema for \( f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 \)

Step 1 Solve \( f'(x) = 0 \)

\[ f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(x-2) \overset{\text{set}}{=\!=} 0 \]

\( \Rightarrow x = 0, 2 \)

Step 2 Determine the sign of \( f''(0) \) and \( f''(2) \)

\[ \begin{gathered} f''(x) = 6x - 6 \\[0.5em] f''(0) = -6 < 0 \\[0.5em] f''(2) = 6 > 0 \end{gathered} \]

Conclusion: \( f \) has a relative maximum at \( x = 0 \) and a relative minimum at \( x = 2 \).

Example 5

Locate and classify all relative extrema for \( f(x) = e^x(x^2 - 5x - 13) \)

  1. Find Crit. Nums.

    \[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= e^x(x^2 - 5x - 13) + e^x(2x - 5) \\ &= e^x(x^2 - 3x - 18) \\ &= e^x(x - 6)(x + 3) \overset{\text{set}}{=\!=} 0 \Rightarrow x = -3, 6 \end{aligned} \]

  2. Compute the sign of \( f''(-3) \) and \( f''(6) \)

    \[ \begin{aligned} f''(x) &= e^x(x^2 - 3x - 18) + e^x(2x - 3) \\ &= e^x(x^2 - x - 21) \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{gathered} f''(-3) = e^{-3}(9 + 3 - 21) = -9e^{-3} < 0 \\[0.5em] f''(6) = e^6(36 - 6 - 21) > 0 \end{gathered} \]

\( \Rightarrow \) rel. max at \( x = -3 \) and rel min at \( x = 6 \)
