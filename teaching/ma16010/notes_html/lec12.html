Lecture 12: Higher Order Derivatives

GOAL: Compute derivatives of derivatives. Interpret the second and third derivative in a physics context.

Summary:

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d^n f}{dx^n} = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{d^{n-1} f}{dx^{n-1}}\right) \\[0.5em] f^{(n)}(x) = [f^{(n-1)}(x)]' \\[0.5em] a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t) \quad\\[0.5em] j(t) = a'(t) = v''(t) = s'''(t) \end{gathered} \]

Recall that the height of a ball tossed straight into the air can be modeled using a parabola. Say

\[ s(t) = -4.9t^2 + 7t + 6 \]

Then the velocity of the ball is given by the derivative.

\[ v(t) = s'(t) = -9.8t + 7 \]

\( v(t) \) is a differentiable function, so we can continue this process

\[ v'(t) = -9.8 \]

Definition The quantity

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{df}{dx}\right) = \frac{d^2 f}{dx^2} \\[0.5em] [f'(x)]' = f''(x) \end{gathered} \]

is called the second derivative of \( f \) with respect to \( x \).

Definition The acceleration of an object, whose position at time \( t \) is given by \( s(t) \), is defined by

\[ a(t) \stackrel{\text{def}}{=} v'(t) = s''(t) \]

Example 1

Find \( f''(x) \) if \( f(x) = 2\sin x + 3\cos x \)

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= 2\sin x + 3\cos x \\ f'(x) &= 2\cos x - 3\sin x \\ f''(x) &= -2\sin x - 3\cos x \end{aligned} \]

NOTE

This is a fun fact (ie, you do not need to know this). If \( A \) and \( B \) are constants, then any function of the form \( f(x) = A\sin x + B\cos x \) satisfies the equation

\[ \frac{d^2 f}{dx^2} + f(x) = 0 \]

Example 2

A particle is traveling along a straight line; its position at time \( t \) is given by

\[ s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t \]

  1. Find the velocity and acceleration of the particle at time \( t \).

    \[ \begin{gathered} v(t) = s'(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 \\[0.5em] a(t) = v'(t) = 6t - 12 \end{gathered} \]

  2. If \( t \) is measured in seconds and \( s(t) \) is measured in meters, find \( v(4) \) and \( a(4) \).

\[ \begin{aligned} v(4) &= 3(4)^2 - 12(4) + 9 \\ &= 48 - 48 + 9 \\ &= 9\ \frac{m}{s} \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} a(t) &= 6(4) - 12 \\ &= 12\ \frac{m/s}{s} \\ &= 12\ \frac{m}{s^2} \end{aligned} \]

  1. What is the acceleration when the velocity is \( 24\ \frac{m}{s} \)?

    Step 1 Find \( t \) when \( v(t) = 24 \)

    \[ \begin{aligned} v(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 &\stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 24 \\ 3t^2 - 12t - 15 &= 0 \\ t^2 - 4t - 5 &= 0 \\ (t+1)(t-5) &= 0 \end{aligned} \]

    Thus, \( t = -1 \) or \( t = 5 \). As \( t \) represents time, we get \( v(t) = 24 \) when \( t = 5 \).

    Step 2 Find \( a(5) \)

    \[ \begin{aligned} a(5) &= 6(5) - 12 \\ &= 30 - 12 \\ &= 18\ \frac{m}{s^2} \end{aligned} \]

Example 3

Suppose the position of a particle is given by

\[ s(t) = \frac{t}{t^2+1} \qquad (t \ge 0) \]

When is the acceleration \( 0 \)?

\[ \begin{aligned} v(t) &= s'(t) \\ &= \frac{(1)(t^2+1) - t(2t)}{(t^2+1)^2} \end{aligned} \]

NOTE:

When you need to take multiple derivatives, it is generally a good idea to simplify as much as possible before you continue.

\[ \begin{aligned} v(t) &= \frac{t^2+1 - 2t^2}{(t^2+1)^2} \\ &= \frac{1-t^2}{(t^2+1)^2} \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} a(t) &= \frac{(-2t)(t^2+1)^2 - (1-t^2)(2)(t^2+1)(2t)}{(t^2+1)^4} \\ &= \frac{-2t(t^2+1)\left[(t^2+1) + (1-t^2)(2)\right]}{(t^2+1)^4} \\ &= \frac{-2t(t^2+1)\left[3-t^2\right]}{(t^2+1)^4} \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{aligned} \]

The denominator is never \( 0 \), so we only need to check when the numerator is \( 0 \). We also disregard any negative solutions as \( t \ge 0 \).

\[ -2t = 0 \quad \text{OR} \quad t^2+1 = 0 \quad \text{OR} \quad 3-t^2 = 0 \] \[ \boxed{t = 0} \qquad \text{Never} \qquad \begin{aligned} &t^2 = 3 \\ &t = \pm\sqrt{3}\\ &\boxed{t =~ \sqrt{3}} \end{aligned} \]

Higher Order Derivatives

As long as the derivative is differentiable, there is nothing stopping us from taking more derivatives.

NOTATION:

3rd Derivative:

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d^3 f}{dx^3} \stackrel{\text{def}}{=\!=} \frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{d^2 f}{dx^2} \right) \\[0.5em] f'''(x) = \left[ f''(x) \right]' \end{gathered} \]

4th Derivative:

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d^4 f}{dx^4} = \frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{d^3 f}{dx^3} \right) \\[0.5em] f''''(x) = \left[ f'''(x) \right]' \end{gathered} \]

nth Derivative:

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d^n f}{dx^n} = \frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{d^{n-1} f}{dx^{n-1}} \right) \\[0.5em] f^{(n)}(x) = \left[ f^{(n-1)}(x) \right]' \end{gathered} \]

NOTE:

In a physics context, the 3rd Derivative of a position function \( s(t) \) is called the jerk at time \( t \), denoted by:

\[ j(t) \stackrel{\text{def}}{=\!=} a'(t) = v''(t) = s'''(t) \]

Example 4

If \( f^{(9)}(x) = 6\sec(2x-5) \), find \( f^{(10)}(x) \).

\[ \begin{aligned} f^{(10)}(x) = \left[ f^{(9)}(x) \right]' &= 6\sec(2x-5)\tan(2x-5)\,(2) \\ &= 12\sec(2x-5)\tan(2x-5) \end{aligned} \]

Example 5

Examine the various derivatives of \( f(x) = \sin x \)

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= \sin x \\ f'(x) &= \cos x \\ f''(x) &= -\sin x \\ f'''(x) &= -\cos x \\ f^{(4)}(x) &= \sin x \end{aligned} \]

So we get a loop:

Example 6

Repeat for the function \( f(x) = x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 \)

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 \\ f'(x) &= 3x^2 + 2x + 1 \\ f''(x) &= 6x + 2 \\ f'''(x) &= 6 \\ f^{(4)}(x) &= 0 \end{aligned} \]

So \(f\) (and polynomials in general) go to \( 0 \) if you take enough (1 + the degree of the polynomial) derivatives

Example 7

Repeat for the function \( f(x) = e^{2x} \)

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= e^{2x} \\ f'(x) &= e^{2x}(2) = 2e^{2x} \\ f''(x) &= 2e^{2x}(2) = 2^2 e^{2x} \\ f'''(x) &= 2^2 e^{2x}(2) = 2^3 e^{2x} \\ &\;\;\vdots \\ f^{(n)}(x) &= 2^{n-1} e^{2x}(2) = 2^n e^{2x} \end{aligned} \]

So we get a never ending chain
