Lecture 22: A Summary of Curve Sketching

Goal: Use the tools of calculus to draw a more accurate graph of a function.

All examples come from §4.5 of James Stewart's Calculus Early Transcendentals, 5th Edition as well as §4.4 of Calculus Early Transcendentals, 3rd Edition by Briggs et. al. (I'm too lazy to make my own examples).

All graphs will be available on Desmos (here).

Guidelines for Sketching a Curve

For drawing the graph of \( y = f(x) \) by hand, consider:

  1. Domain: Find all values where \( f \) is defined.
  2. Intercepts: Find the \( y \)-intercept by computing \( f(0) \). Find the \( x \)-intercepts by solving the equation \( f(x) = 0 \).
  3. Symmetry:
    1. Even functions: When \( f(x) = f(-x) \); this means the graph is symmetric about the \( y \)-axis.
    2. Odd functions: When \( f(-x) = -f(x) \); this means it is symmetric about the origin (the left side is a \( 180^\circ \) rotation of the right side)
    3. Periodic functions: When \( f(x+p) = f(x) \) for some \( p > 0 \), the smallest such \( p \) is called the period. You only need to focus on one period of the function.
  4. Asymptotes and End Behavior:
    1. Vertical Asymptotes: Occurs at \( x = a \) when either: \[ \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \ \text{OR} \ \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) \]

      is not finite (equals \( \infty \) or \( -\infty \)).

    2. Horizontal Asymptotes: Occurs at \( y = L \) when either: \[ \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = L \quad \text{OR} \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L \]
    3. Slant Asymptotes: The line \( y = mx + b \) is a slant asymptote when either : \[ \lim_{x \to -\infty} [f(x) - (mx+b)] = 0 \quad \text{OR} \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} [f(x) - (mx+b)] = 0 \]

      For rational functions, if the quotient from long division \( q(x) \) is linear, then \( q(x) \) is the slant asymptote.

  5. Intervals of Increase or Decrease: Use the I/D Test to see when \( f \) is increasing or decreasing. If \( f' > 0 \), then \( f \) is increasing. If \( f' < 0 \), \( f \) is decreasing.
  6. Relative Maximum and Minimum Values: Use the 1st or 2nd derivative test to determine if critical numbers are relative extrema.
  7. Concavity and Inflection Points: Use the concavity test. If \( f'' > 0 \), then \( f \) is concave upwards. If \( f'' < 0 \), \( f \) is concave downwards.
  8. If needed, get more information: You can also compute \( (x, f(x)) \) pairs to see the height of the graph. The tangent line also tells you where the graph is going at that point.

Example 1 (Why Graphing Calculators Can Be Deceiving).

Sketch a graph of \( f(x) = \frac{1}{3}x^3 - 400x \)

Domain: \( (-\infty, \infty) \)

y-int: \( f(0) = 0 \). Ie, it passes through the origin.

x-int:

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{1}{3}x^3 - 400x \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] x^3 - 1200x = 0 \\[0.5em] x(x^2 - 1200) = 0 \end{gathered} \] \[ \implies \begin{array}{l} x = 0, \pm\sqrt{1200} \\ x \approx 0, \pm 34.6 \end{array} \]

End Behavior:

\[ \begin{gathered} \lim_{x \to \infty} \left[ \frac{1}{3}x^3 - 400x \right] = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{3}x^3 = \infty \\[0.5em] \lim_{x \to -\infty} \left[ \frac{1}{3}x^3 - 400x \right] = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{1}{3}x^3 = -\infty \end{gathered} \]

ID and C Test:

\[ \begin{gathered} f(x) = \frac{1}{3}x^3 - 400x \\[0.5em] f'(x) = x^2 - 400 \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] x = \pm\sqrt{400} \\[0.5em] x = \pm 20 \\[0.5em] f''(x) = 2x \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

Example 2. Sketch a graph of \( f(x) = \frac{2x^2}{x^2 - 1} \)

Domain: \( (-\infty, -1) \cup (-1, 1) \cup (1, \infty) = \mathbb{R} - \{-1, 1\} \)

y-int:

\[ f(0) = \frac{2(0)^2}{0^2 - 1} = 0 \]

x-int:

\[ \begin{gathered} 2x^2 \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

Ie, \(f\) passes through the origin

End Behavior:

\[ \begin{gathered} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2x^2}{x^2 - 1} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2x^2}{x^2} = 2 \\[0.5em] \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{2x^2}{x^2 - 1} = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{2x^2}{x^2} = 2 \end{gathered} \]

Asymptotes:

\[ \begin{gathered} x^2 - 1 \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] \implies x = \pm 1 \end{gathered} \]

Thus, there are VAs at \( x = -1 \) and \( x = 1 \). Moreover, from the previous work, \(f\) has a HA at \( y = 2 \).

I/D and C Test:

\[ \begin{gathered} f(x) = \frac{2x^2}{x^2 - 1} \\[0.5em] f'(x) = \frac{4x(x^2 - 1) - 2x^2(2x)}{(x^2 - 1)^2} = \frac{-4x}{(x^2 - 1)^2} \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] -4x = 0 \\[0.5em] x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

Ie, \( x = 0 \) is our only critical number.

\[ \begin{gathered} f''(x) = \frac{-4(x^2-1)^2 - (-4x)(2)(x^2-1)(2x)}{(x^2-1)^4} \\[0.5em] = \frac{-4(x^2-1) + 16x^2}{(x^2-1)^3} \\[0.5em] = \frac{12x^2 + 4}{(x^2-1)^3} \end{gathered} \]

\( f'' \) is never 0 and well-defined (in the domain of \( f \)). However, concavity can still change at the asymptotes, so we include them in our sign chart.

Example 3. Sketch a graph of \( f(x) = \frac{x^3}{x^2 + 1} \)

Domain: \( (-\infty, \infty) \)

Intercepts: \( (0,0) \)

Asymptotes and End Behavior: There are no VAs and HAs, but there is a slant asymptote. Doing the long division,

\[ \begin{array}{r} x \phantom{xxxxxx} \\ x^2+1 \overline{) \begin{array}[t]{l} x^3 \phantom{xx} \\ -\,(x^3+x) \\ \hline \phantom{xxx} -x \end{array}} \end{array} \]

We have \( y = x \) for a slant asymptote.

I/D and C Test:

\[ \begin{gathered} f(x) = \frac{x^3}{x^2+1} \\[0.5em] f'(x) = \frac{3x^2(x^2+1) - x^3(2x)}{(x^2+1)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{3x^4 + 3x^2 - 2x^4}{(x^2+1)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{x^4 + 3x^2}{(x^2+1)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{x^2(x^2+3)}{(x^2+1)^2} \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{gathered} \]

\( f'(x) = 0 \) at \( x = 0 \), but \( x^2 \), \( x^2 + 3 \), and \( (x^2+1)^2 \) are always positive anywhere except 0. So \( f \) is always increasing by the I/D Test.

\[ \begin{gathered} f'(x) = \frac{x^4 + 3x^2}{(x^2+1)^2} \\[0.5em] f''(x) = \frac{(4x^3+6x)(x^2+1)^2 - (x^4+3x^2)(2)(x^2+1)(2x)}{(x^2+1)^4} \\[0.5em] = \frac{2x(3-x^2)}{(x^2+1)^3} \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] 2x(3-x^2) = 0 \\[0.5em] \Rightarrow \quad x = 0, \pm\sqrt{3} \end{gathered} \]

We make a sign chart for \( f'' \)

Test Point \( -100 \) \( -1 \) \( 1 \) \( 100 \)
Sign of \( (x^2+1)^3 \) \( + \) \( + \) \( + \) \( + \)
Sign of \( 2x \) \( - \) \( - \) \( + \) \( + \)
Sign of \( 3 - x^2 \) \( - \) \( + \) \( + \) \( - \)
Sign of \( f'' \) \( + \) \( - \) \( + \) \( - \)
C Test CU CD CU CD

So, there are IPs at

\[ (1)\quad (\pm\sqrt{3},\ f(\pm\sqrt{3})) = \left(\pm\sqrt{3},\ \pm\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{3}\right) \approx (\pm 1.7,\ \pm 1.299) \]

Example 4. Sketch a graph of \( f(x) = \frac{10x^3}{x^2 - 1} \)

Domain: Since there are VAs at \( x = -1 \) and \( x = 1 \), the domain is \( (-\infty, -1) \cup (-1, 1) \cup (1, \infty) \)

Intercepts: \( f \) only passes through the origin \( (0,0) \).

End Behavior and Asymptotes: As mentioned earlier, by solving \( x^2 - 1 = 0 \) one has there are VAs at \( x = \pm 1 \).

There are no HAs as there is a Slant asymptote. Doing the long division,

\[ \begin{array}{r} 10x \phantom{{}^3} \\ x^2-1 \overline{)\,10x^3 \phantom{{}-10x}} \\ \underline{-\,(10x^3-10x)} \\ 10x \end{array} \]

we get \( y = 10x \) as a slant asymptote (again, it is the quotient we look at to determine the equation of the slant asymptote).

ID and C Test:

\[ \begin{gathered} f'(x) = \frac{30x^2(x^2-1) - 10x^3(2x)}{(x^2-1)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{10x^4 - 30x^2}{(x^2-1)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{10x^2(x^2-3)}{(x^2-1)^2} \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] \Rightarrow x = 0, \pm\sqrt{3} \\[0.5em] f''(x) = \frac{(40x^3 - 60x)(x^2-1)^2 - (10x^4 - 30x^2)2(x^2-1)(2x)}{(x^2-1)^4} \\[0.5em] = \frac{20x(x^2+3)}{(x^2-1)^3} \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] \Rightarrow x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

So we want to include our critical numbers, possible locations for IPs, and the asymptotes in our sign chart

Example 5 ( Bell Curve ). Sketch a graph of \( f(x) = e^{-x^2} \)

Domain: \( (-\infty, \infty) \)

Intercepts: No x-int. The y-intercept is \( (0,1) \)

Asymptotes: There are no vertical nor slant asymptotes. However,

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} e^{-x^2} = \lim_{x \to -\infty} e^{-x^2} = 0 \]

So \( f \) has a HA at \( y = 0 \)

ID and C Test:

\[ \begin{gathered} f(x) = e^{-x^2} \\[0.5em] f'(x) = -2x e^{-x^2} \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] -2x = 0 \\[0.5em] \Longrightarrow \quad x = 0 \\[0.5em] f''(x) = -2 e^{-x^2} + (-2x)(-2x) e^{-x^2} \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] -2e^{-x^2}\left[1 - 2x^2\right] = 0 \\[0.5em] \Longrightarrow \quad x = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \end{gathered} \]

Example 6. Sketch a graph of \( f(x) = \ln(4 - x^2) \)

Domain: When

\[ \begin{gathered} 4 - x^2 > 0 \\[0.5em] 4 > x^2 \end{gathered} \]

Ie, the domain is \( (-2, 2) \)

Intercepts: The y-intercept is \( f(0) = \ln 4 \) and the x-intercepts occur when \( 4 - x^2 = 1 \), or \( x = \pm\sqrt{3} \)

Asymptotes and “End Behavior”:

We can't take the limit as \( x \to \pm\infty \) for this function. The equivalent with be taking \( x \) to the edge of the domain. Ie,

\[ \begin{gathered} \lim_{x \to -2^+} \ln(4 - x^2) = -\infty \\[0.5em] \lim_{x \to 2^-} \ln(4 - x^2) = -\infty \end{gathered} \]

implying \( f \) has VAs at \( x = \pm 2 \).

ID and C Test:

\[ \begin{gathered} f(x) = \ln(4 - x^2) \\[0.5em] f'(x) = \frac{-2x}{4 - x^2} \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] \Longrightarrow \quad x = 0 \\[0.5em] f''(x) = \frac{-2(4 - x^2) - (-2x)(-2x)}{(4 - x^2)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{-8 + 2x^2 - 4x^2}{(4 - x^2)^2} \\[0.5em] = \frac{-2x^2 - 8}{(4 - x^2)^2} \\[0.5em] = -\left[ \frac{x^2 + 8}{(4 - x^2)^2} \right] \end{gathered} \]

What is in brackets is always positive in the domain of \( f \). Hence, \( f'' \) is always negative implying \( f \) is CD for all \( x \) in the domain. We still need to make a sign chart for \( f' \).

Sign Chart for \( f' \)
\( (-2, 0) \) \( (0, 2) \)
Test Point \( -1 \) \( 1 \)
Sign of \( f' \) \( + \) \( - \)
ID Test Inc Dec

Therefore, there is a relative maximum at \( (0, f(0)) = (0, \ln 4) \).
