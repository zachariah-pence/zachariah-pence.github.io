The Point of Diminishing Returns

(This will not show up on any HW or Exam)

Definition. When the marginal cost/revenue/profit obtains an absolute maximum, this is called the point of diminishing returns.

Example 1. (Example 5 in Lecture 25)

A company uses the following function:

\[ R(x) = 10x^2 - \frac{2}{3}x^3; \quad 0 \le x \le 10 \]

to model revenue after spending \( x \) million dollars on advertising. Find and interpret the point of diminishing returns.

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{Objective: maximize } \frac{dR}{dx} = 20x - 2x^2 \\[0.5em] \text{Given} : x \in [0,10] \\[0.5em] \frac{dR}{dx} = 20x - 4x \\[0.5em] \frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{dR}{dx} \right) = 20 - 4x \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] 4x = 20 \\[0.5em] x = 5 \end{gathered} \]

By comparing the output values

Output values of \( \frac{dR}{dx} \) at \( x = 0, 5, 10 \)
\( x \) 0 5 10
\( \frac{dR}{dx} \) 0 50 0

we see the marginal revenue has an absolute maximum at \( x = 5 \). Therefore, that is the point of diminishing returns.

Conclusion: The point of diminishing returns is at \( \$5 \) million. Ie, before \( \$5 \) millions, each additional spent in advertising brought in more revenue than the previous one. However, at \( \$5 \) million, the rate that revenue increases by spending more on advertising starts to slow.
