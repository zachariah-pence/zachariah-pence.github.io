    \[ f(x) = \ln \sqrt[4]{\frac{4+4x}{4-x}} \]\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= \ln \left[ \frac{4+4x}{4-x} \right]^{\frac{1}{4}} \\ &= \frac{1}{4} \ln \left[ \frac{4+4x}{4-x} \right] \\ &= \frac{1}{4} \left[ \ln(4+4x) - \ln(4-x) \right] \\ &= \frac{1}{4} \ln(4+4x) - \frac{1}{4} \ln(4-x) \end{aligned} \]\[ f'(x) = \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{4+4x} - \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{(-1)}{4-x} \]\[ \begin{aligned} f'(1) &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{8} + \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{3} \\ &= \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{12} \\ &= \frac{3}{24} + \frac{2}{24} \\ &= \frac{5}{24} \end{aligned} \]

  1. Problem 2. Find the initial acceleration of a particle if its position after \( t \) seconds is given by:

    \[ s(t) = \ln(t^2 + 1) \]

    Express your final answer in \( \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2} \).

    Ie, we need to find \( s''(0) \).

    \[ \begin{gathered} v(t) = s'(t) = \frac{2t}{t^2+1} \\[0.5em] a(t) = s''(t) = \frac{2(t^2+1) - 2t(2t)}{(t^2+1)^2} \\[0.5em] a(0) = \frac{2-0}{(0+1)^2} = 2\,\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2} \end{gathered} \]

  1. Problem 3. Given the algebraic curve \( x^2 + 4xy + y^2 = 13 \). What is the equation of the line tangent to the curve at the point \( (x,y) = (2,1) \)?

    Find \( \left. \frac{dy}{dx} \right|_{(x,y)=(2,1)} \):

    \[ \begin{gathered} x^2 + 4xy + y^2 = 13 \\[0.5em] \frac{d}{dx}\left( x^2 + 4xy + y^2 \right) = \frac{d}{dx}(13) \\[0.5em] 2x + 4y + 4x\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \\[0.5em] 4x\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = -2x - 4y \\[0.5em] \left[ 4x + 2y \right] \frac{dy}{dx} = -2x - 4y \\[0.5em] \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2x - 4y}{4x + 2y} \\[0.5em] \left. \frac{dy}{dx} \right|_{(2,1)} = \frac{-2(2) - 4(1)}{4(2) + 2(1)} \\[0.5em] = -\frac{8}{10} \\[0.5em] = -\frac{4}{5} \end{gathered} \]

    Equation for a line with slope \( -\frac{4}{5} \) Containing \( (2,1) \):

    \[ \begin{gathered} y - 1 = -\frac{4}{5}(x - 2) \\[0.5em] y = -\frac{4}{5}x + \frac{8}{5} + 1 \\[0.5em] y = -\frac{4}{5}x + \frac{13}{5} \end{gathered} \]

  1. Problem 4. A baseball diamond is a square with 90 ft on each side. A player runs from first base to second base at 13 ft/sec. At what rate is the player's distance from home base increasing when he is half way from first to second base?

    Let \( y \) be the distance from 1st base to the player and \( z \) the distance from home plate to the player. We know \( \frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \) and we need to know \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) when \( y = 45 \). \( y \) and \( z \) are related by the Pythagorean Theorem:

    \[ \begin{gathered} 90^2 + y^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] \frac{d}{dt}\left[ 90^2 + y^2 \right] = \frac{d}{dt}\left[ z^2 \right] \\[0.5em] 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 2z\frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{y}{z}\frac{dy}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

    We also need to determine \( z \) when \( y = 45 \). As \( z \ge 0 \),

    \[ \begin{aligned} z &= \sqrt{90^2 + y^2} \\ &= \sqrt{90^2 + (45)^2} \\ &= \sqrt{90^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}90^2} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &= \sqrt{\tfrac{5}{4} \cdot 90^2} \\ &= 45\sqrt{5} \end{aligned} \]

Hence, when \( y = 45 \):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dz}{dt} &= \frac{y}{z}\frac{dy}{dt} \\ &= \frac{45}{45(\sqrt{5})}(13) \\ &= \frac{13}{\sqrt{5}} \end{aligned} \]

The distance from home plate and the player is increasing at a rate of \( \frac{13}{\sqrt{5}} \frac{m}{s} \).

  1. Problem 5. (OPTIONAL...very optional) Repeat Problem 4, but replace 13ft/s with an arbitrary speed \( v \) ft/s and 90ft with an arbitrary length \( \ell \) ft.

    Let \( y \) be the distance from 1st base to the player and \( z \) the distance from home plate to the player. We know \( \frac{dy}{dt} = v \) and we need to know \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) when \( y = \frac{1}{2}\ell \). \( y \) and \( z \) are related by the Pythagorean Theorem:

    \[ \begin{gathered} \ell^2 + y^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] \frac{d}{dt}\left[ \ell^2 + y^2 \right] = \frac{d}{dt}\left[ z^2 \right] \\[0.5em] 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 2z\frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{y}{z}\frac{dy}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

    We also need to determine \( z \) when \( y = \frac{1}{2}\ell \). As \( z \ge 0 \),

    \[ \begin{aligned} z &= \sqrt{\ell^2 + y^2} \\ &= \sqrt{\ell^2 + \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\ell\right)^2} \\ &= \sqrt{\ell^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}\ell^2} \\ &= \sqrt{\tfrac{5}{4}\ell^2} \\ &= \tfrac{1}{2}\ell\sqrt{5} \end{aligned} \]

Hence, when \( y = \frac{1}{2}\ell \):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dz}{dt} &= \frac{y}{z}\frac{dy}{dt} \\ &= \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\ell}{\tfrac{1}{2}\ell(\sqrt{5})}(v) \\ &= \frac{v}{\sqrt{5}} \end{aligned} \]

The distance from home plate and the player is increasing at a rate of \( \frac{v}{\sqrt{5}} \frac{m}{s} \).

  1. Problem 6. Sand pours onto a surface at 19 cm3/s, forming a conical pile with a base diameter that is always equal to the pile's altitude. How fast is the altitude of the pile increasing when the pile is 5 cm high?

    [NOTE: The volume of a cone with base radius \( r \) and height \( h \) is \( V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \)]

    Let \( r, h, \) and \( V \) be the radius, height, and volume of the conical pile, respectively. We know \( \frac{dV}{dt} = 19 \) and we want to know \( \frac{dh}{dt} \) when \( h = 5 \). We can relate \( r, h, \) and \( V \) by

    \[ V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \]

    However, taking the derivative as is, we would need to use the product rule and hence we will have \( \frac{dr}{dt} \) in our expression. So, we use the "base diameter ... is always equal to the pile's altitude" clause to rewrite \( V \) solely as a function of \( h \). In symbols, the clause translates to \( 2r = h \), or \( r = \frac{h}{2} \). Hence,

    \[ \begin{aligned} V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \\ V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 h \\ V &= \frac{1}{12}\pi h^3 \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= \frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{1}{12}\pi h^3 \right) \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dV}{dt} &= \frac{1}{12}\pi\left(3h^2\frac{dh}{dt}\right) \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= \frac{\pi}{4}h^2\frac{dh}{dt} \\ \frac{dh}{dt} &= \frac{4}{\pi h^2}\frac{dV}{dt} \end{aligned} \]

Thus, when \( h = 5 \),

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dh}{dt} &= \frac{4}{\pi(5)^2}(19) \\ \frac{dh}{dt} &= \frac{76}{25\pi} \end{aligned} \]

The height of the pile is increasing at a rate of \( \frac{76}{25\pi} \frac{cm}{s} \).

  1. Problem 7. Find the critical numbers of \( y = x^2 e^x \).

    We need to determine when \( y' \) is 0 or undefined:

    \[ \begin{aligned} y' &= 2x e^x + x^2 e^x \\ &= x e^x (2 + x) \end{aligned} \]

    \( y' \) is never undefined, so we solve

    \[ x e^x (2 + x) \stackrel{\text{set}}{=} 0 \]

    to get \( x \) is either 0 or \( -2 \).
