Lecture 15: Related Rates (Trigonometric Relations)

Assume all variables are functions of time.

1 Pythagorean Relations

As we saw in last lecture, if quantities are related, we can see how their derivatives are related.

We already saw some examples of how variables can be related. For examples, the perimeter, area, surface area, or volume of a shape. But, these are far from the only examples.

For another example, suppose \( x, y, \) and \( z \) are the side lengths of a right triangle.

Problem 1.

Suppose \( x = x(t) \), \( y = y(t) \), and \( z = z(t) \) are related by the Pythagorean Theorem. So,

\[ x^2 + y^2 = z^2 \]

How are \( \frac{dx}{dt} \), \( \frac{dy}{dt} \), and \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) related? In particular, what happens when \( \frac{dz}{dt} = 0 \)?

Like last time, we see how the derivatives of \( x, y, \) and \( z \) are related by taking the derivative of both sides of \( x^2 + y^2 = z^2 \).

\[ \begin{gathered} x^2 + y^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] 2x \frac{dx}{dt} + 2y \frac{dy}{dt} = 2z \frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} = z \frac{dz}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

In particular, if \( \frac{dz}{dt} = 0 \).

\[ x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \]

Problem 2.

A ladder 10m long rests against a vertical wall. If the bottom of the ladder slides away from the wall at a rate of 1 m/s, how fast is the top of the ladder sliding down the wall when the bottom of the ladder is 6m from the wall?

As with any Related Rates problem, we run through the checklist (see Lecture 14).

  1. Let \( x \) be the displacement from the wall to the bottom of the ladder. Let \( y \) be the displacement from the floor to the top of the ladder.
  2. Yes, \( x^2 + y^2 = 10^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dx}{dt} = 1 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dy}{dt} \) when \( x = 6 \). As we will see later, we also NTK \( y \) when \( x = 6 \).
\[ \begin{gathered} x^2 + y^2 = 10^2 \\[0.5em] 2x \frac{dx}{dt} + 2y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \\[0.5em] x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \\[0.5em] y \frac{dy}{dt} = -x \frac{dx}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y} \frac{dx}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Find \( y \) when \( x = 6 \): As \( y > 0 \),

\[ \begin{gathered} y = \sqrt{10^2 - x^2} \\[0.5em] y\big|_{x=6} = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} \\[0.5em] = \sqrt{100 - 36} \\[0.5em] = \sqrt{64} \\[0.5em] = 8 \end{gathered} \]

So,

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y} \frac{dy}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dy}{dt}\bigg|_{\substack{x=6 \\ x'=1}} = -\frac{6}{8}(1) \\[0.5em] = -\frac{6}{8} \\[0.5em] = -\frac{3}{4} \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the top of the ladder is falling at a rate of \( \frac{3}{4} \) m/s.

Problem 3.

Two cars leave from the same point at the same time. Car A is traveling east at 50km/h and car B is traveling north at 60km/h. At what rate is the distance between the two cars changing when car A is 0.3km and car B is 0.4km from their starting point?

Run through the checklist:

  1. Let \( x \) and \( y \) be Car A's and B's distance from their starting point. Let \( z \) be the distance between Car A and B.
  2. \( x^2 + y^2 = z^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dx}{dt} = 50 \) and \( \frac{dy}{dt} = 60 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) when \( x = 0.3 \) and \( y = 0.4 \). Also, the value of \( z \) when \( x = 0.3 \) and \( y = 0.4 \).
\[ \begin{gathered} x^2 + y^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] 2x \frac{dx}{dt} + 2y \frac{dy}{dt} = 2z \frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{1}{z}\left[ x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} \right] \end{gathered} \]

Find \( z \):

Since \( z > 0 \), \( z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \).

\[ \begin{gathered} z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \\[0.5em] z\bigg|_{\substack{x=0.3 \\ y=0.4}} = \sqrt{(0.3)^2 + (0.4)^2} \\[0.5em] = \sqrt{0.25} \\[0.5em] = 0.5 \end{gathered} \]

Now,

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{1}{z}\left[ x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} \right] \\[0.5em] = \frac{1}{0.5}\left[ 0.3(50) + 0.4(60) \right] \\[0.5em] = 78 \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the distance between cars A and B is increasing at a rate of 78 \( \frac{km}{h} \).

Problem 4.

A plane flying horizontally at an altitude of 1 km and a speed of 500km/h passes directly over a radar station. Find the rate at which the distance from the plane to the station is increasing when it is 2km away from the station.

Run through the check list:

  1. Let \( z \) denote the distance from the plane and the radar station and \( x \) the horizontal distance.
  2. \( x^2 + 1^2 = z^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dx}{dt} = 500 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) (and \( x \)) when \( z = 2 \).
\[ \begin{gathered} x^2 + 1^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] 2x \frac{dx}{dt} = 2z \frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{x}{z} \frac{dx}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Find \( x \): When \( x > 0 \), \( x = \sqrt{z^2 - 1} \). So, when \( z = 2 \).

\[ \begin{gathered} x\big|_{z=2} = \sqrt{2^2 - 1} \\[0.5em] = \sqrt{3} \end{gathered} \]

So,

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{x}{z} \frac{dx}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt}\bigg|_{\substack{z=2 \\ z'=500}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(500) \\[0.5em] = 250\sqrt{3} \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the distance between the plane and the radar station is increasing at a rate of \( 250\sqrt{3} \) \( \frac{km}{h} \).

Problem 5.

A Little League baseball diamond is a square with 60ft on each side. A player runs from 1st base to 2nd base at 12ft/s. At what rate is the player's distance from home plate increasing if he is halfway between 1st and 2nd base?

Run through the checklist:

  1. Let \( y \) be the distance from 1st Base to the player, and \( z \) the distance from home to the player.
  2. \( 60^2 + y^2 = z^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dy}{dt} = 12 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) (and \( z \)) when \( y = \frac{1}{2}(60) = 30 \).
\[ \begin{gathered} 60^2 + y^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] 2y \frac{dy}{dt} = 2z \frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{y}{z} \frac{dz}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Find \( z \): Similar to other problems, when \( y = 30 \)

\[ z = \sqrt{60^2 + 30^2} = 30\sqrt{5} \]

Thus,

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{y}{z} \frac{dy}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dz}{dt}\bigg|_{\substack{y=30 \\ y'=12}} = \frac{30}{30\sqrt{5}}(12) \\[0.5em] = \frac{12}{\sqrt{5}} \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the distance between the player and home base is increasing at a rate of \( \frac{12}{\sqrt{5}} \) \( \frac{ft}{s} \).

Problem 6.

A boat is pulled into a dock by a rope attached to the bow of the boat and passing through a pulley on the dock that is 1m higher than the bow of the boat. If the rope is pulled in at a rate of 1m/s, how fast is the boat approaching the dock when it is 8m from the dock?

Run through the checklist:

  1. Let \( z \) be the length of the rope and \( x \) the boat's distance from the dock.
  2. \( x^2 + 1^2 = z^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dz}{dt} = -1 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dx}{dt} \) (and \( z \)) when \( x = 8 \)
\[ \begin{gathered} x^2 + 1^2 = z^2 \\[0.5em] x \frac{dx}{dt} = z \frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{z}{x} \frac{dz}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Find \( z \):

\[ \begin{gathered} z = \sqrt{x^2 + 1} \\[0.5em] z\big|_{x=8} = \sqrt{64 + 1} \\[0.5em] = \sqrt{65} \end{gathered} \]

So,

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{z}{x} \frac{dz}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dx}{dt}\bigg|_{\substack{z'=-1 \\ x=8}} = \frac{\sqrt{65}}{8}(-1) \\[0.5em] = -\frac{\sqrt{65}}{8} \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the boat is approaching the dock at a rate of \( \sqrt{65}/8 \) \( \frac{m}{s} \).

2 Trigonometric Relations

Problem 7.

A man walks along a straight path at a speed of 4 ft/s. A searchlight is located on the ground 20 ft from the path and is kept focused on the man. At what rate is the searchlight rotating when the man is 15ft from the point on the path closest to the searchlight?

Run through the checklist:

  1. Let \( x \) be the horizontal distance from the man and the search light and \( \theta \) the angle between the man, the search light, and the closest point on the man's path closest to the search light (see Figure 7).
  2. \( \frac{x}{20} = \tan\theta \), or \( x = 20\tan\theta \).
  3. K: \( \frac{dx}{dt} = 4 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{d\theta}{dt} \) (and \( \cos\theta \)) when \( x = 15 \).
\[ \begin{gathered} x = 20\tan\theta \\[0.5em] \frac{dx}{dt} = 20\sec^2\theta\, \frac{d\theta}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{1}{20} \cos^2\theta\, \frac{dx}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Find \( \cos\theta \) when \( x = 15 \):

\[ \begin{gathered} x = 20\tan\theta \\[0.5em] 15 = 20\tan\theta \\[0.5em] \tan\theta = \frac{15}{20} \\[0.5em] \tan\theta = \frac{3}{4} \end{gathered} \]

From the right triangle,

We get \( \cos\theta = \frac{4}{5} \)

So,

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{1}{20} \cos^2\theta\, \frac{dx}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{d\theta}{dt}\bigg|_{\substack{x=15 \\ x'=4}} = \frac{1}{20}\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^2 (4) \\[0.5em] = \frac{16}{125} \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the searchlight is rotating clockwise at a rate of \( \frac{16}{125} \) \( \frac{\mathrm{rad}}{\mathrm{s}} \).

Problem 8.

Two sides of a triangle are 4m and 5m in length and the angle between them is increasing at a rate of 0.06rad/s. Find the rate at which the area of the triangle is increasing when the angle between the sides of fixed length is \( \frac{\pi}{3} \).

Run through the checklist:

  1. Let \( \theta \) denote the interior angle of the fixed side lengths of the triangle and \( A \) the area of the triangle.
  2. \( A = \frac{1}{2} bh = \frac{1}{2}(4)(5\sin\theta) = 10\sin\theta \)
  3. K: \( \frac{d\theta}{dt} = 0.06 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) when \( \theta = \frac{\pi}{3} \)
\[ \begin{gathered} A = 10\sin\theta \\[0.5em] \frac{dA}{dt} = 10\cos\theta\, \frac{d\theta}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dA}{dt}\bigg|_{\substack{\theta=\pi/3 \\ \theta'=0.06}} = 10\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)(0.06) \\[0.5em] = 10\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{6}{100} \\[0.5em] = \frac{3}{10} \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, the area of the triangle is increasing at a rate of \( \frac{3}{10} \) \( \frac{m^2}{s} \).
