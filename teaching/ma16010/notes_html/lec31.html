Lecture 31: The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Goal: Evaluate integrals with more complicated integrands.

Recall Our main tool for computing integrals so far has been geometry.

Example 1.

Compute \( \int_{-2}^{4} 3x \, dx \)

\[ \begin{gathered} \int_{-2}^{4} 3x \, dx = \frac{1}{2}(12)(4) - \frac{1}{2}(2)(6) \\[0.5em] = 24 - 6 \\[0.5em] = 18 \end{gathered} \]

This approach won't work if we wanted to compute something like \( \int_{1}^{3} e^{x} \, dx \). To remedy this, we ask the following:

Question 1: If \( v(t) \) represents the velocity of an object at time \( t \), what is the meaning of the symbol \( \int_{a}^{b} v(t) \, dt \)?

\[ \text{Velocity} = \frac{\Delta \text{Position}}{\Delta \text{Time}} \implies \text{Velocity}(\Delta \text{Time}) = \Delta \text{Position} \]

So each of these rectangles approximates the change in position after a change in time.

As \( \Delta t \to 0 \) (# of rectangles \( \to \infty \)).

\[ \int_{a}^{b} v(t) \, dt = \text{The net change in position from } t = a \text{ to } t = b. \]

Question 2: Can we rewrite \( \int_{a}^{b} v(t) \, dt \) in terms of position?

\[ \begin{gathered} \begin{bmatrix} \text{Stopping} \\ \text{Point} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \text{Starting} \\ \text{Point} \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} \text{The change in} \\ \text{position ("displacement")} \end{bmatrix} \\[0.5em] s(b) = s(a) + \int_{a}^{b} v(t) \, dt \\[0.5em] \int_{a}^{b} v(t) \, dt = s(b) - s(a) \end{gathered} \]

Theorem (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC)). Let \( f \) be a continuous function on \( [a, b] \). Then,

\[ \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = F(b) - F(a) \]

where \(F\) is any antiderivative of \( f \).

Lots of Little Examples

Example 2.

Verify \( \int_{-2}^{4} 3x \, dx = 18 \) using the FTC.

An antiderivative for \( 3x \) is \( \frac{3}{2} x^{2} \). So,

\[ \int_{-2}^{4} 3x \, dx = \frac{3}{2}(4)^{2} - \frac{3}{2}(-2)^{2} = 24 - 6 = 18 \]

Example 3.

Compute \( \int_{1}^{3} e^{x} \, dx \)

An antiderivative for \( e^{x} \) is just \( e^{x} \). So,

\[ \int_{1}^{3} e^{x} \, dx = e^{3} - e \]

Remark. What happens when we pick another antiderivative?

Any antiderivative of \( e^{x} \) is of the form \( e^{x} + C \) for a constant \( C \). So,

\[ \int_{1}^{3} e^{x} \, dx = (e^{3} + C) - (e^{1} + C) = e^{3} - e \]

The constants cancel. For this reason, we just pick the “simplest” antiderivative (when \( C = 0 \)).

Remark (Notation). We often write:

\[ \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = F(x)\Big|_{a}^{b} = F(x)\Big]_{a}^{b} = \left[ F(x) \right]_{a}^{b} = F(b) - F(a) \]

Example 4.

Compute \( \int_{3}^{6} \frac{dx}{x} = \int_{3}^{6} \frac{1}{x} \, dx. \)

\[ \int_{3}^{6} \frac{1}{x} \, dx = \ln|x| \Big|_{3}^{6} = \ln|6| - \ln|3| = \ln 6 - \ln 3 = \ln 2 \]

Example 5.

Compute \( \int_{-10}^{-5} \frac{1}{x^{2}} \, dx. \)

\[ \int_{-10}^{-5} \frac{1}{x^{2}} \, dx = -\frac{1}{x} \Big|_{-10}^{-5} = \left( -\frac{1}{-5} \right) - \left( -\frac{1}{-10} \right) = \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{10} = \frac{1}{10} \]

Example 6.

Compute \( \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x \, dx. \)

\[ \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x \, dx = -\cos x \Big|_{0}^{\pi} = \left( -\cos \pi \right) - \left( -\cos 0 \right) = 1 - (-1) = 2 \]

Remark. Recall \( \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = - \int_{b}^{a} f(x) \, dx \). So in this previous example,

\[ -\cos x \Big|_{0}^{\pi} = \cos x \Big|_{\pi}^{0} \]

So for computations, this is a way of absorbing negative signs.

More Complicated Examples

Example 7.

Compute \( \int_{0}^{1} \left( 3 + x\sqrt{x} \right) \, dx \)

  1. METHOD 1: Use Linearity

    \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{1} \left( 3 + x\sqrt{x} \right) dx &= \int_{0}^{1} \left( 3 + x^{\frac{3}{2}} \right) dx \\ &= 3 \int_{0}^{1} 1 \, dx + \int_{0}^{1} x^{\frac{3}{2}} \, dx \\ &= 3 \left[ x \right]_{0}^{1} + \left[ \frac{2}{5} x^{\frac{5}{2}} \right]_{0}^{1} \\ &= 3 \left[ 1 - 0 \right] + \left[ \frac{2}{5} - 0 \right] \\ &= 3 + \frac{2}{5} \\ &= \frac{17}{5} \end{aligned} \]

  2. METHOD 2:

    \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{1} \left( 3 + x\sqrt{x} \right) dx &= \int_{0}^{1} \left( 3 + x^{\frac{3}{2}} \right) dx \\ &= \left[ 3x + \frac{2}{5} x^{\frac{5}{2}} \right]_{0}^{1} \\ &= \left[ 3 + \frac{2}{5} \right] - \left[ 0 + 0 \right] \\ &= \frac{17}{5} \end{aligned} \]

Example 8.

Compute \( \int_{1}^{2} \left( \frac{4+t^{2}}{t^{3}} \right) dt \)

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{1}^{2} \left( \frac{4+t^{2}}{t^{3}} \right) &= \int_{1}^{2} \left( \frac{4}{t^{3}} + \frac{t^{2}}{t^{3}} \right) dt = \int_{1}^{2} \left( \frac{4}{t^{3}} + \frac{1}{t} \right) dt \\ &= \left[ -\frac{2}{t^{2}} + \ln|t| \right]_{1}^{2} = \left[ \ln|t| - \frac{2}{t^{2}} \right]_{1}^{2} \\ &= \left[ \ln 2 - \frac{1}{2} \right] - \left[ 0 - 2 \right] \\ &= \ln 2 + \frac{3}{2} \end{aligned} \]

Example 9.

Compute \( \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \sec x \left( \sec x + \cos x \right) \, dx \)

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \sec x \left( \sec x + \cos x \right) dx &= \int_{0}^{\pi/4} \left( \sec^{2} x + 1 \right) dx \\ &= \left[ \tan x + x \right]_{0}^{\pi/4} \\ &= \left[ \tan \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{4} \right] - \left[ \tan 0 + 0 \right] \\ &= 1 + \frac{\pi}{4} \end{aligned} \]

Often integration problems (especially in MA 16020 and 261) are phrased as follows:

Example 10.

Find the area of the region bounded by \( y = e^{x+1} \), \( y = 0 \), \( x = -1 \), and \( x = 1 \).

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{-1}^{1} e^{x+1} \, dx &= \int_{-1}^{1} e \cdot e^{x} \, dx \\ &= e \int_{-1}^{1} e^{x} \, dx \\ &= e \left[ e^{x} \right]_{-1}^{1} \\ &= e \left[ e - e^{-1} \right] \\ &= e^{2} - 1 \end{aligned} \]

Example 11.

Find the area of the region bounded by \( y = x^{2} - x - 6 \), \( y = 0 \), \( x = -2 \), and \( x = 3 \).

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{-2}^{3} \left( x^{2} - x - 6 \right) dx &= \left[ \frac{1}{3} x^{3} - \frac{1}{2} x^{2} - 6x \right]_{-2}^{3} \\ &= \left[ 9 - \frac{9}{2} - 18 \right] - \left[ -\frac{8}{3} - 2 + 12 \right] \\ &= \left[ 9 - 18 + 2 - 12 \right] + \left[ \frac{8}{3} - \frac{9}{2} \right] \\ &= -19 - \frac{11}{6} \\ &= \frac{-114}{6} - \frac{11}{6} \\ &= \frac{-125}{6} \end{aligned} \]

Computing Unsigned Areas

As an aside, remember \( \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx \) computes the signed area between the graph of \( f \) and the \( x \)-axis on \( [a, b] \). If we wanted to find the total area (ignoring the sign), we would need a way to treat the negative area as positive area. In symbols, the unsigned area between the graph of \( f \) and the \( x \)-axis on \( [a, b] \) is \( \int_{a}^{b} |f(x)| \, dx \).

Example 12.

Consider the region bounded by \( f(x) = 6x^{3} - 6x^{2} - 12x \), \( y = 0 \), \( x = -1 \), and \( x = 2 \).

  1. Find the net (signed) area;

    \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{-1}^{2} \left( 6x^{3} - 6x^{2} - 12x \right) dx &= \left[ \frac{3}{2} x^{4} - 2x^{3} - 6x^{2} \right]_{-1}^{2} \\ &= \left[ 24 - 16 - 24 \right] - \left[ \frac{3}{2} + 2 - 6 \right] \\ &= -\frac{27}{2} \end{aligned} \]

  2. Find the total (unsigned) area.

    \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{-1}^{2} |f(x)| \, dx &= \int_{-1}^{0} f(x) \, dx + \int_{0}^{2} -f(x) \, dx\\ &= \int_{-1}^{0} \left( 6x^{3} - 6x^{2} - 12x \right) dx + \int_{0}^{2} \left( -6x^{3} + 6x^{2} + 12x \right) dx \\ &= \frac{5}{2} + 16\\&= \frac{37}{2} \end{aligned} \]
