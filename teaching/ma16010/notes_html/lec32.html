Lecture 32: The Net Change Theorem

Goal: Interpret the role of the (definite) integral in applications.

Theorem (Net Change Theorem). The integral of a rate of change is the net change. In symbols, if \( t_0 \) is the “starting time” and \( t_1 \) is the “stopping time”, then

\[ \int_{t_0}^{t_1} f'(t)\ dt = f(t_1) - f(t_0) \qquad\qquad f(t_1) = f(t_0) + \int_{t_0}^{t_f} f'(t)\ dt \]

In other words, definite integrals can be interpreted as the accumulation of change.

Definition. The displacement of an object on an interval \( [t_0, t_1] \) is the net change in position on \( [t_0, t_1] \). By the NTC,

\[ \text{Displacement} = S(t_1) - S(t_0) = \int_{t_0}^{t_1} v(t)\, dt \]

Example 1.

Let \( v(t) = (3t-5)\text{m/s} \) be the velocity of an object that is moving along a straight line.

  1. Find the object's displacement after 3 seconds.

    Choose coordinate system so \( S(0) = 0 \). With this, the displacement is:

    \[ \begin{gathered} S(3) = S(3) - S(0) = \int_{0}^{3} v(t)\, dt = \int_{0}^{3} (3t-5)\, dt = \left[\tfrac{3}{2}t^2 - 5t\right]_{0}^{3} \\[0.5em] = \left[\tfrac{3}{2}\cdot 3^2 - 5\cdot 3\right] - [0] = \tfrac{27}{2} - 15 = -\tfrac{3}{2}\ \text{meters} \end{gathered} \]

    Ie, \( \tfrac{3}{2} \) meters left of the starting point.

  2. After the object starts moving, how long does it take to return to its starting place?

    We solve this problem using the NTC, let \( s(t) \) be the position at time \( T \). By the NTC,

    \[ \begin{gathered} s(t) = S(0) + \int_{0}^{T} v(t)\, dt = 0 + \int_{0}^{T} (3t-5)\, dt = \left[\tfrac{3}{2}t^2 - 5t\right]_{0}^{T} \\[0.5em] = \tfrac{3}{2}T^2 - 5T \end{gathered} \]

    Finding the positive root of \( \tfrac{3}{2}T^2 - 5t = 0 \), we see it takes \( T = \tfrac{10}{3} \) seconds for the object to return to its starting point.

Example 2.

A car moves north at 40mph from 2 to 4PM, then moves south at 30mph from 4 to 5PM. Find the net displacement of the car.

The velocity of the car \( t \) hours past noon is given by

\[ v(t) = \begin{cases} 40 & 2 \le t \le 4 \\ -30 & 4 \le t \le 5 \end{cases} \]

The net displacement is,

\[ \begin{gathered} S(5) - S(2) \overset{\text{NTC}}{=\!=} \int_{2}^{5} v(t)\, dt = \int_{2}^{4} v(t)\, dt + \int_{4}^{5} v(t)\, dt \\[0.5em] = \int_{2}^{4} 40\, dt + \int_{4}^{5} (-30)\, dt \\[0.5em] = 40\,[t]_{2}^{4} - 30\,[t]_{4}^{5} \\[0.5em] = 40\,(4-2) - 30\,(5-4) \\[0.5em] = 80 - 30 \\[0.5em] = 50 \text{ miles north of the starting point} \end{gathered} \]

Example 3.

The acceleration \( a(t) \) of a car after the driver steps on the brake, before the car comes to a full stop, is \( a(t) = -(t-5)^2 \) where \( t \) is the time in seconds and the acceleration is measured in mph per second. What is the decrease in velocity 3 seconds after applying the brakes?

We need to find \( v(3) - v(0) \)

\[ \begin{gathered} v(3) - v(0) \overset{\text{NTC}}{=\!=} \int_{0}^{3} -(t-5)^2\, dt \\[0.5em] = \int_{0}^{3} (-t^2 + 10t - 25)\, dt \\[0.5em] = \left[ -\tfrac{1}{3}t^3 + 5t^2 - 25t \right]_{0}^{3} \\[0.5em] = \left[ -\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 3^3 + 5\cdot 3^2 - 25\cdot 3 \right] - [0] \\[0.5em] = -9 + 45 - 75 \\[0.5em] = -39 \end{gathered} \]

So the velocity decreased by 39 mph in the first 3 seconds.

Example 4.

If the motor on a motorboat consumes gasoline at a rate of \( V'(t) = (5 - 0.1t^3)\frac{\text{gal}}{hr} \), how much gasoline was consumed in the first 2 hours?

We need to find \( V(2) - V(0) \).

\[ \begin{gathered} V(2) - V(0) \overset{\text{NTC}}{=\!=} \int_{0}^{2} V'(t)\, dt \\[0.5em] = \int_{0}^{2} (5 - 0.1t^3)\, dt \\[0.5em] = \left[ 5t - \tfrac{0.1}{4}t^4 \right]_{0}^{2} \\[0.5em] = \left[ 5t - 0.025t^4 \right]_{0}^{2} \\[0.5em] = 5\cdot 2 - 0.025\cdot 16 - [0 - 0] \\[0.5em] = 10 - 0.4 \\[0.5em] = 9.6 \end{gathered} \]

Thus, 9.6 gallons of gasoline was consumed.

Example 5.

A population grows at a rate of \( P'(t) = \sqrt{t}(100t + 5100) \) people per year, where \( t \) represents the number of years since 2000. How much did the population increase from 2004 to 2009?

We need to compute \( P(9) - P(4) \).

\[ \begin{gathered} P(9) - P(4) = \int_{4}^{9} P'(t)\, dt = \int_{4}^{9} \sqrt{t}\,(100t + 5100)\, dt \\[0.5em] = \int_{4}^{9} \left(100t^{\frac{3}{2}} + 5100t^{\frac{1}{2}}\right) dt \\[0.5em] = \left[ 100\cdot \tfrac{2}{5}t^{\frac{5}{2}} + 5100\cdot \tfrac{2}{3}t^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_{4}^{9} \\[0.5em] = \left[ 40\left(\sqrt{t}\right)^{5} + 3400\left(\sqrt{t}\right)^{3} \right]_{4}^{9} \\[0.5em] = \left[ 40\cdot 3^{5} + 3400\cdot 3^{3} \right] - \left[ 40\cdot 2^{5} + 3400\cdot 2^{3} \right] \\[0.5em] = 101520 - 28480 \\[0.5em] = 73040 \text{ people} \end{gathered} \]

\( 3\overline{)5100} = 1700 \)

Example 6.

Water is pumped into a storage tank at a rate of \( r(t) = 250 - 5t \) liters per minute (where \( 0 \le t \le 50 \)). If there are 5000 liters of water in the tank initially and the tank has a maximum capacity of 7000 liters, will the tank overflow if we run the pump for 10 minutes?

If \( V \) is the amount of water in the tank at time \( t \), we need to find \( V(10) \). By the NTC,

\[ \begin{gathered} R(10) = R(0) + \int_{0}^{10} r(t)\, dt = 5000 + \int_{0}^{10} (250 - 5t)\, dt \\[0.5em] = 5000 + \left[ 250t - \tfrac{5}{2}t^2 \right]_{0}^{10} \\[0.5em] = 5000 + \left[ 250\cdot 10 - \tfrac{5}{2}\cdot 100 \right] - [0] \\[0.5em] = 5000 + 2500 - 250 \\[0.5em] = 7250 \\[0.5em] > 7000 \end{gathered} \]

So, yes, the tank over flows. In fact, solving

\[ V(T) = 5000 + \int_{0}^{T} (250 - 5t)\, dt = 7000 \]

we see the tank over flows roughly 8.77 minutes after turning on the pump

Example 7.

The marginal cost of manufacturing \( x \) yards of a certain fabric is \( C'(x) = 5 - 0.008x + 0.000009x^2 \) (in dollars per yard). Find the increase in cost if the production level is raised from 1000 yards to 3000 yards.

We need to compute \( C(3000) - C(1000) \)

\[ \begin{gathered} C(3000) - C(1000) \overset{\text{NTC}}{=\!=} \int_{1000}^{3000} C'(x)\, dx \\[0.5em] = \int_{1000}^{3000} \left[ 5 - 0.008x + 0.000009x^2 \right] dx \\[0.5em] = \left[ 5x - 0.004x^2 + 0.000003x^3 \right]_{1000}^{3000} \\[0.5em] = \left[ 15000 - 36000 + 81000 \right] - \left[ 5000 - 4000 + 3000 \right] \\[0.5em] = \$56000 \end{gathered} \]

Definition. The density of an object at a point is the derivative of mass with respect to volume. If your object is thin enough to be approximated using a line segment, then the density at a point \( x \) is given by:

\[ \rho(x) \overset{\text{def}}{=\!=} \frac{dm}{dx} \]

Example 8.

The density of a thin, 2 meter wire at a point \( x \) is \( \rho(x) = 1 + x^2 \) grams per meter. Find the mass of the wire.

Let \( m(x) \) be the mass of the part of the rod represented by the interval \( [0, x] \). We need to compute \( m(2) - m(0) \):

\[ \begin{gathered} m(2) - m(0) \overset{\text{NTC}}{=\!=} \int_{0}^{2} m'(x)\, dx = \int_{0}^{2} \rho(x)\, dx \\[0.5em] = \int_{0}^{2} (1 + x^2)\, dx \\[0.5em] = \left[ x + \tfrac{1}{3}x^3 \right]_{0}^{2} \\[0.5em] = 2 + \tfrac{8}{3} \\[0.5em] = \tfrac{14}{3} \text{ grams} \end{gathered} \]

Example 9.

The change in the concentration of a product during a chemical reaction is given by \( C'(t) = 3t^2 - 4t + 2 \) moles per liter per second. If the initial concentration was 1 mole per liter, what is the concentration after 3 seconds?

We need to find \( C(3) \)

\[ \begin{gathered} C(3) = C(0) + \int_{0}^{3} C'(t)\, dt = \int_{0}^{3} \left[ 3t^2 - 4t + 2 \right] dt \\[0.5em] = 1 + \left[ t^3 - 2t^2 + 2t \right]_{0}^{3} \\[0.5em] = 1 + \left[ 27 - 18 + 6 \right] \\[0.5em] = 1 + 15 \\[0.5em] = 16 \ \tfrac{\text{mol}}{\text{L}} \end{gathered} \]
