Lecture 19: The Extreme Value Theorem

Goal: Be able to compute the maximum and minimum of a differentiable function on a closed and bounded domain.

Definition:

Let \( f \) be a function with domain \( D \):

  1. We say \( f \) has an absolute (or global) maximum at \( c \) if \( f(x) \le f(c) \) for all \( x \) in \( D \).
  2. Likewise, \( f \) has an absolute minimum at \( c \) if \( f(c) \le f(x) \) for all \( x \) in \( D \).

But, they can have one and not the other.

They can also have neither

Question: Is there a way to guarantee that a function has both an absolute maximum and minimum?

Answer: Yes! ... if we make a few assumptions.

Theorem (Extreme Value Theorem)

Let \( f \) be a function defined on a closed interval \( [a,b] \)

  1. If \( f \) is continuous on \( [a,b] \), then \( f \) has both an absolute maximum and minimum.
  2. If \( f \) is differentiable on \( (a,b) \), then there are only 3 places the absolute maximum and minimum can occur:
    1. \( x = a \)
    2. \( x = b \)
    3. At a critical number on \( (a,b) \)

NOTE (Why (1)?)

The main idea is the endpoints \( (a, f(a)) \) are \( (b, f(b)) \) are fixed. So, if there wasn't a maximum, the graph would skyrocket to infinity, but needs to return to \( (b, f(b)) \). That can not be done without breaking continuity.

In other words, we can find an absolute max and min if we have a continuous function on a closed interval. But, if our function is differentiable, there is a procedure to find it. We will demonstrate this in the examples.

Example 1

Find the maximum and minimum of \( f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 1 \) on \( \left[-\tfrac{1}{2}, 4\right] \)

Step 1 Find the critical numbers

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 3x^2 - 6x \\ &= 3x(x-2) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{aligned} \]\[ \Rightarrow x = 0, 2 \]

Step 2 Compute the value of \( f \) at the endpoints and at each critical number

Values of \( f(x) \) at the endpoints and critical numbers
\( x \) \( -\tfrac{1}{2} \) \( 0 \) \( 2 \) \( 4 \)
\( f(x) \) \( \tfrac{1}{8} \) \( 1 \) \( -3 \) \( 17 \)

Step 3 State Conclusion

Conclusion: \( f \) has a minimum value of \( -3 \) at \( x = 2 \) and a maximum value of \( 17 \) at \( x = 4 \)

Example 2

Find the maximum and minimum for \( f(x) = x^4 - 2x^2 + 3 \) on \( [0,2] \)

Step 1 Critical numbers in \( [0,2] \)

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 4x^3 - 4x = 4x(x^2-1) \\ &= 4x(x-1)(x+1) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{aligned} \]\[ \Rightarrow x = 0, 1 \]

NOTE:

\( x = -1 \) is also a critical number for \( f \). However, it is not in the interval \( [0,2] \), so it is not taken into consideration.

Step 2

Values of \( f(x) \) at the endpoints and critical numbers
\( x \) \( 0 \) \( 1 \) \( 2 \)
\( f(x) \) \( 3 \) \( 2 \) \( 11 \)

Conclusion: \( f \) has a minimum value of \( 2 \) at \( x = 1 \) while it has a maximum value of \( 11 \) at \( x = 2 \).

Example 3

Find the maximum and minimum of \( f(x) = \sin x + \cos x \) on \( \left[0, \frac{\pi}{3}\right] \)

Step 1

\[ \begin{gathered} f'(x) = \cos x - \sin x \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] \cos x = \sin x \\[0.5em] \tan x = 1 \end{gathered} \]

The only solution on \( \left[0, \frac{\pi}{3}\right] \) is \( x = \frac{\pi}{4} \)

Step 2

Values of \( f(x) \) at the endpoints and critical number
\( x \) \( 0 \) \( \pi/4 \) \( \frac{\pi}{3} \)
\( f(x) \) \( 1 \) \( \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414 \) \( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \approx 1.366 \)

Conclusion: \( f \) has a minimum of \( 1 \) at \( x = 0 \) and a maximum of \( \sqrt{2} \) at \( x = \frac{\pi}{4} \)

Example 4 (HW 19, Q 8)

Find the absolute extrema of \( f(x) = 5x^2 e^x + 8 \) on \( [-4, 2] \)

Step 1

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 10xe^x + 5x^2 e^x \\ &= 5x e^x (2 + x) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{aligned} \] \[ \Rightarrow x = 0, -2 \]

Step 2

Values of \( f(x) \) at the endpoints and critical numbers
\( x \) \( -4 \) \( -2 \) \( 0 \) \( 2 \)
\( f(x) \) \( \frac{80}{e^4} + 8 \approx 9.465 \) \( \frac{20}{e^2} + 8 \approx 10.707 \) \( 8 \) \( 20e^2 + 8 \approx 155.78 \)

Conclusion: \( f \) has an absolute min at \( (0, 8) \) and an absolute max at \( (2, 20e^2 + 8) \).

When \( f \) has Only One Critical Number

Fact: Regardless of the interval, if \( f \) has only one critical number \( c \). Then, if \( f \) has a relative extremum at \( c \), it is an absolute extremum.

NOTE (Why?)

Since it is a relative extremum, if it was not absolute, then to get more extreme the graph would have to "bend" meaning \( f \) would have more than one critical number.

Example 5

Consider \( f(x) = x^2 + 2x \) on \( (-2, 0) \)

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 2x + 2 \\ &= 2(x+1) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{aligned} \]

Ie, \( x = -1 \) is the only critical number. From a sign chart

Sign chart for \( f' \)
\( (-2, -1) \) \( (-1, 0) \)
Test Point \( -\frac{3}{2} \) \( -\frac{1}{2} \)
Sign of \( f' \) \( - \) \( + \)

\( f \) has a relative minimum by the 1st Derivative Test. By the previous fact, \( f \) has an absolute minimum on \( (-2, 0) \) at \( x = -1 \).

NOTE:

The work above tells us nothing about an absolute max. In fact, it does not exist. We can see this by looking at the graph of \( f \) on \( (-2, 0) \).

Example 6

Consider \( y = \frac{1}{4x^2 + 3} \) on \( [-1, 1] \)

\[ \begin{aligned} y' &= -\frac{1}{(4x^2+3)^2}(8x) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\ -8x &= 0 \\ x &= 0 \end{aligned} \]

So \( x = 0 \) is the only critical number. From the sign chart

Sign chart for \( y' \)
\( [-1, 0) \) \( (0, 1) \)
Test Point \( -\frac{1}{2} \) \( \frac{1}{2} \)
Sign chart for \( f' \)
Sign of \( f' \) \( + \) \( - \)

\( y \) has a relative (hence absolute) max at \( 0 \) by the 1st Derivative Test. We can not say anything about a minimum without extra work.
