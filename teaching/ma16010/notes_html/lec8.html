Lecture 8 The Product Rule

Goal Be able to differentiate fcns of the form \( f(x) g(x) \)

Question: Does \( \frac{d}{dt}(f(t) g(t)) = \frac{d}{dt}(f(t)) \cdot \frac{d}{dt}(g(t)) \)?

Answer: No!

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d}{dt}(1 \cdot t) = 1 \\[0.5em] \frac{d}{dt}(1) \cdot \frac{d}{dt}(t) = 1 \cdot 0 = 0 \end{gathered} \]

Let's take a tiny step in time:

\[ \Delta(f(t) g(t)) = \text{Area of Shaded Region} \] \[ = \Delta f(t) \cdot g(t) + f(t) \Delta g(t) + \left[ \begin{array}{c} \text{Something} \\ \to 0 \text{ as} \\ \Delta t \to 0 \end{array} \right] \]

Divide by \( \Delta t \)

\[ \frac{\Delta(f(t) g(t))}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta f(t)}{\Delta t} g(t) + f(t) \frac{\Delta g(t)}{\Delta t} + [\text{"}] \]

Take \( \Delta t \to 0 \)

Theorem (Product Rule)

Let \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \) be differentiable functions Then.

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d}{dx}(f(x) g(x)) = \frac{df}{dx} \cdot g(x) + f(x) \cdot \frac{dg}{dx} \\[0.5em] [f(x) g(x)]' = f'(x) g(x) + f(x) g'(x) \end{gathered} \]

"left""right""right d-left plus left right"

Example 1

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2 \\[0.5em] \frac{d}{dx}(x^3) = \frac{d}{dx}(x \cdot x^2) = [x]' \cdot x^2 + x \cdot [x^2]' \\[0.5em] = 1 \cdot x^2 + x \cdot 2x \\[0.5em] = x^2 + 2x^2 = 3x^2 \checkmark \end{gathered} \]

NOTE:

This gives us a way of computing \( \frac{d}{dx}(x^n) \) for an integer \( n \ge 0 \) by continuing Ex. 1.

\[ \begin{gathered} x^4 = x \cdot x^3 \\[0.5em] x^5 = x \cdot x^4 \\[0.5em] \vdots \end{gathered} \]

Ie, this shows the Power Rule is true for non negative integer powers.

Example 2

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d}{dx}(2 \sin x \cos x) = [2 \sin x]' \cos x + 2 \sin x [\cos x]' \\[0.5em] = 2 \cos x \cos x + 2 \sin x (-\sin x) \\[0.5em] = 2 [\cos^2 x - \sin^2 x] \end{gathered} \]

Example 3

Let \( h(x) = \underbrace{(x^{10} + x + 1)}_{f} \underbrace{(x^2 + 9)}_{g} \)

  1. Compute \( h'(x) \)

    \[ \begin{gathered} f(x) = x^{10} + x + 1 \longrightarrow f'(x) = 10x^9 + 1 \\[0.5em] g(x) = x^2 + 9 \longrightarrow g'(x) = 2x \\[0.5em] h'(x) = f'(x) g(x) + f(x) g'(x) \\[0.5em] = (10x^9 + 1)(x^2 + 9) + (x^{10} + x + 1)(2x) \end{gathered} \]

  2. Compute \( h'(1) \)

    \[ h'(1) = 11 \cdot 10 + 3 \cdot 2 = 116 \]

Example 4

Compute \( h'(x) \) if \( h(x) = (x+1)(2x+1)(3x+1) \)

\[ \begin{gathered} h'(x) = [x+1]'(2x+1)(3x+1) + (x+1)\left[ (2x+1)(3x+1) \right]' \\[0.5em] = (2x+1)(3x+1) + (x+1)\left[ [2x+1]'(3x+1) + (2x+1)[3x+1]' \right] \\[0.5em] = (2x+1)(3x+1) + (x+1)\left[ 2(3x+1) + (2x+1)\cdot 3 \right] \\[0.5em] = 1 \cdot (2x+1)(3x+1) + (x+1)\cdot 2 \cdot (3x+1) + (x+1)(2x+1)\cdot 3 \end{gathered} \]

Example 5

Find the equation of the line tangent to

\[ \begin{gathered} h(x) = (\sqrt{x} + 4)\left( x^{\frac{3}{4}} - 2x \right) \quad \text{at} \quad x = 16 \\[0.5em] f(x) = \sqrt{x} + 4 \qquad f'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \\[0.5em] f(16) = 4 + 4 = 8 \qquad f'(16) = \frac{1}{2\cdot 4} = \frac{1}{8} \\[0.5em] g(x) = x^{\frac{3}{4}} - 2x \qquad g'(x) = \frac{3}{4} x^{-\frac{1}{4}} - 2 \\[0.5em] g(16) = 8 - 32 = -24 \qquad = \frac{3}{4 \cdot \sqrt[4]{x}} - 2 \\[0.5em] g'(16) = \frac{3}{8} - 2 = -\frac{13}{8} \\[0.5em] \text{Slope}: \; h'(16) = f'(16) g(16) + f(16) g'(16) \\[0.5em] = \frac{1}{8}(-24) + 8\left( \frac{-13}{8} \right) = -3 - 13 = -16 \\[0.5em] \text{Point}: \; x\text{-coord}: \; x = 16 \\[0.5em] y\text{-coord}: \; h(16) = f(16) g(16) = 8(-24) = -192 \\[0.5em] \text{Eq}: \; y + 192 = -16(x - 16) \implies y = -16x + (256 - 192) \\[0.5em] \boxed{y = -16x + 64} \end{gathered} \]

Example 6

When does \( y = 10x^5 e^x \) have a horizontal tangent line?

\[ \begin{gathered} y' = 50x^4 e^x + 10x^5 e^x \\[0.5em] = 10x^4 e^x (5+x) \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\[0.5em] 10x^4 = 0 \quad \text{OR} \quad e^x = 0 \quad \text{OR} \quad 5 + x = 0 \\[0.5em] \boxed{x = 0} \qquad \text{Never} \qquad \boxed{x = -5} \end{gathered} \]

Example 7

Compute \( h'(x) \) if

\[ \begin{gathered} h(x) = 6e^x \sin x - 13 e^x \cos x \\[0.5em] = e^x (6 \sin x - 13 \cos x) \\[0.5em] \implies h'(x) = e^x (6 \sin x - 13 \cos x) + e^x (6 \cos x + 13 \sin x) \\[0.5em] = e^x (6 \sin x - 13 \cos x + 6 \cos x + 13 \sin x) \\[0.5em] = e^x (19 \sin x - 7 \cos x) \end{gathered} \]
