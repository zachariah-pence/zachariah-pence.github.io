Lecture 14: Related Rates (Perimeter/Area/Volume Relations)

Goal: If a quantity changes over time, explain how its derivative changes over time.

1 Introduction (Circles)

Problem 1.

A circle's area and radius are related by the equation:

\[ A = \pi r^2 \]

If \( A \) are \( r \) are changing as time advances, is there any relation between \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) and \( \frac{dr}{dt} \)?

That is, suppose we have a circle of radius \( r \):

Then if we let a little time pass, there will be a change in the radius. As a result, there will be a change to the area.

To describe \( \frac{dA}{dt} \), we recognize \( A \) and \( r \) are functions of time and differentiate both sides of the equation \( A = \pi r^2 \)

\[ \begin{aligned} A &= \pi r^2 \\ A_{(t)} &= \pi \left[ r_{(t)} \right]^2 \\ \frac{d}{dt}\left( A_{(t)} \right) &= \frac{d}{dt}\left( \pi \left[ r_{(t)} \right]^2 \right) \\ \frac{dA}{dt} &= \pi \left( 2 \left[ r_{(t)} \right] \frac{dr}{dt} \right) \\ \frac{dA}{dt} &= 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} \end{aligned} \]

Notice that \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) is a function of both \( \frac{dr}{dt} \) (the change in the radius) and \( r \) (the radius itself).

\( \frac{dr}{dt} \) being large causes a large change in area

Even if \( \frac{dr}{dt} \) is small, if the radius is large, it will cause a large change in area.

Problem 2.

(a) In the previous example, if \( r = 2 \) and \( \frac{dr}{dt} = 3 \), then what is the value of \( \frac{dA}{dt} \)? Interpret.

Given

\[ \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} \]

we plug in the appropriate values

\[ \begin{gathered} \left. \frac{dA}{dt} \right|_{\substack{r = 2 \\ r' = 3}} = 2\pi (2)(3) \\[0.5em] = 12\pi \end{gathered} \]

Therefore, when the radius is 2 units long, the area is increasing at a rate of \( 12\pi \) units\( ^2 \)/sec.

(b) In the previous example, if \( r = 1 \) and \( \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi \), then what is the value of \( \frac{dr}{dt} \)?

METHOD 1:

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} \\[0.5em] \frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{1}{2\pi r} \frac{dA}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Thus,

\[ \begin{gathered} \left. \frac{dr}{dt} \right|_{\substack{r = 1 \\ A' = 2\pi}} = \frac{1}{2\pi (1)} (2\pi) \\[0.5em] = 1 \end{gathered} \]

METHOD 2:

At this point, you can plug in the numbers and solve for \( \frac{dr}{dt} \).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dA}{dt} &= 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} \\ (2\pi) &= 2\pi (1) \frac{dr}{dt} \\ 2\pi &= 2\pi \frac{dr}{dt} \\ \frac{dr}{dt} &= 1 \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, if the radius is 1 unit long, in order for the area to change at a rate of \( 2\pi \) units\( ^2 \)/sec, the radius will need to change at a rate of \( 1 \frac{\text{unit}}{\text{sec}} \).

Problem 3.

The radius of a circle r is increasing at a constant rate 3 cm/min.

(a) Find the rate of change of the area of the circle (\( A \)) when the radius is 5cm.

We want to translate from English to the language of math. To aid with this, it is a good idea to draw a picture if you can. So, in this case a circle of radius \( r \):

Now, we need to determine the following from the word problem:

  1. What are the quantities in play?
    • We see the circle's radius and area mentioned. So, let \( r \) and \( A \) be the radius and area respectively.
  2. Is there a formula that relates all of our quantities?
    • Yes, namely, \( A = \pi r^2 \)
  1. What do we know?
    • "r is increasing at a constant rate 3 \( \frac{\text{cm}}{\text{min}} \)." Ie, \( \frac{dr}{dt} = 3 \).
  2. What do we need to know?
    • "the rate of change of the area... when the radius is 5." Ie, we need to know \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) when \( r = 5 \).

Now, we use the relation is (2) to find \( \frac{dA}{dt} \):

\[ \begin{gathered} A = \pi r^2 \\[0.5em] \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} \end{gathered} \]

Now, when \( r = 5 \), \( \frac{dr}{dt} = 3 \), so we have enough information to find \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) when \( r = 5 \).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dA}{dt} &= 2\pi(5)(3) \\ \frac{dA}{dt} &= 30\pi \end{aligned} \]

If we want to be prim and proper, our final step is to translate back into English. So, our final answer is something like:

"When the radius is 5 cm, the area of the circle is increasing at a rate of \( 30\pi \) cm\( ^2 \)/min."

(b) Find the rate of change of the circumference of the circle (\( C \)) when the radius is 5cm.

Run through the checklist:

  1. The circle's radius and circumference are mentioned. Let \( r \) and \( C \) denote the radius and circumference respectively.
  2. Formula: \( C = 2\pi r \)
  3. What do we need to know? We NTK \( \frac{dC}{dt} \) when \( r = 5 \)
  4. What do we know? \( \frac{dr}{dt} = 3 \)

NOTE:

  • A common strategy is figuring out what we need to find (Ie, answer (4)), then work backwards. Hence, why the list is out of order
  • Your quantities may go in (3) or (4) depending on preference. For example, I say "we need to know \( \frac{dC}{dt} \) when \( r = 5 \)," but it is also valid to say "when we know \( r = 5 \), we need to know \( \frac{dC}{dt} \)."

Now,

\[ \begin{aligned} C &= 2\pi r \\ \frac{dC}{dt} &= 2\pi \frac{dr}{dt} \\ \left.\frac{dC}{dt}\right]_{r=5} &= 2\pi (3) \\ &= 6\pi \end{aligned} \]

When the radius is 5 cm, the circumference is increasing at a rate of \( 6\pi \) \( \frac{cm}{min} \).

2 Rectangular Prisms

Problem 4.

The edges of a cube are shrinking at a rate of \( 10 \) cm/s.

(a) How fast is the volume (\( V \)) shrinking when each side length is 9cm long?

NOTE:

I will abbreviate "What do we know?" with "K" and "What do we need to know?" with "NTK" from now on.

Run through the check list:

  1. The box's side length and Volume
    • \( x = \) Length of the side
    • \( V = \) Volume
  2. \( V = x^3 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dx}{dt} = -10 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dV}{dt} \) when \( x = 9 \)

So,

\[ \begin{aligned} V &= x^3 \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= 3x^2 \frac{dx}{dt} \end{aligned} \]

NOTE:

In general, you need to wait to plug in numbers until you are done taking derivatives. Otherwise, you may not get the correct expression for \( \frac{dV}{dt} \). Plugging in numbers too early is a very common mistake.

\[ \begin{aligned} \left.\frac{dV}{dt}\right]_{\substack{x=9 \\ x'=-10}} &= 3(9)^2(-10) \\ \left.\frac{dV}{dt}\right]_{\substack{x=9 \\ x'=-10}} &= -2430 \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, when the side lengths are 9 cm long, the volume is decreasing at a rate of \( 2430 \) \( \frac{cm^3}{s} \).

(b) How fast is the surface area (\( A \)) shrinking when each side length is 9cm long?

Run through the checklist:

  1. If \( x \) is as in Part (a), let \( A \) denote the surface area of the cube.
  2. Formula: \( A = 6x^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dx}{dt} = -10 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) when \( x = 9 \)
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= 6x^2 \\ \frac{dA}{dt} &= 12x \frac{dx}{dt} \\ \left.\frac{dA}{dt}\right]_{\substack{x=9 \\ x'=-10}} &= 12(9)(-10) \\ \left.\frac{dA}{dt}\right]_{\substack{x=9 \\ x'=-10}} &= -1080 \end{aligned} \]

When the side lengths of the cube are 9 cm long, the surface area of the cube is decreasing at a rate of \( 1080 \) \( \frac{cm^2}{s} \).

3 Spheres

Problem 5.

A balloon is (roughly) a sphere. The balloon deflates and its radius decreases at a rate of 2 cm/s.

(a) How fast is the volume (\( V \)) shrinking when the radius is 5cm long?

Run through the checklist:

  1. The balloon's radius and volume are mentioned. Denote them with \( r \) and \( V \) respectively.
  2. Formula: \( V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dr}{dt} = -2 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dV}{dt} \) when \( r = 5 \)
\[ \begin{aligned} V &= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt} \\ \left.\frac{dV}{dt}\right]_{\substack{r=5 \\ r'=-2}} &= 4\pi (5)^2 (-2) \\ \left.\frac{dV}{dt}\right]_{\substack{r=5 \\ r'=2}} &= -200\pi \end{aligned} \]

When its radius is 5 cm, the balloon's volume is decreasing at a rate of \( 200 \pi\) \( \frac{cm^3}{s} \).

(b) How fast is the surface area (\( A \)) shrinking when the radius is 5cm long?

Run through the checklist:

  1. \( r \) is as in Part (a). Let \( A \) denote the surface area of the balloon.
  2. Formula: \( A = 4\pi r^2 \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dr}{dt} = -2 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dA}{dt} \) when \( r = 5 \)
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= 4\pi r^2 \\ \frac{dA}{dt} &= 8\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} \\ \left.\frac{dA}{dt}\right]_{\substack{r=5 \\ r'=-2}} &= 8\pi (5)(-2) \\ \left.\frac{dA}{dt}\right]_{\substack{r=5 \\ r'=-2}} &= -80\pi \end{aligned} \]

When the radius of the balloon is 5 cm long, its surface area is decreasing at a rate of \( 80\pi \) \( \frac{\mathrm{cm}^2}{\mathrm{s}} \).

4 Cylinders

Problem 6.

A cylindrical tank with a radius and height of 100 cm stands upright. Water is being drained at a rate of \( 7cm^3/s \). How fast is the water level changing when the tank is half empty?

Run through the check list

  1. Let \( h \) denote the "water level" and \( V \) denote the amount of water in the tank.
  2. Formula: \( V = \pi (100)^2 h = 10000\pi h \)
  3. K: The radius and height are a constant 100 cm. We also know \( \frac{dV}{dt} = -7 \)
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dh}{dt} \) when \( h = \frac{1}{2}(100) = 50 \)

NOTE:

Take a moment to verify why we can plug in 100 for the tank's height and radius now as opposed to after we take derivatives. They are truly constant (not changing with time) as opposed to the height and volume of the water.

\[ \begin{aligned} V &= 1000\pi h \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= 1000\pi \frac{dh}{dt} \\ \frac{dh}{dt} &= \frac{1}{1000\pi} \frac{dV}{dt} \end{aligned} \]

When \( h = 50 \), \( \frac{dV}{dt} = -7 \), so

\[ \begin{aligned} \left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right]_{h=50} &= \frac{1}{1000\pi}(-7) \\ \left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right]_{h=50} &= \frac{-7}{10000\pi} \ \frac{\mathrm{cm}}{\mathrm{s}} \\ \left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right]_{h=50} &\approx -2.2 \ \frac{\mathrm{mm}}{\mathrm{s}} \end{aligned} \]

So, when the tank is half way full, the water level is decreasing at a rate of roughly 2.2 \( \frac{\mathrm{mm}}{\mathrm{s}} \).

5 Cones

Problem 7.

Sand pours onto a surface at \( 15cm^3/s \), forming a conical pile with a base diameter that is always equal to the pile's altitude. How fast is the altitude of the pile increasing when the pile is 8cm high?

Run through the check list:

  1. Let \( V \) denote the volume of the pile, along with \( h \) and \( r \) being the height and radius respectively.
  2. Formula: \( V = \frac{\pi}{3} r^2 h \)
  3. K: \( \frac{dV}{dt} = 15 \), also \( 2r = h \).
  4. NTK: \( \frac{dh}{dt} \) when \( h = 8 \)

NOTE:

We know nothing about \( \frac{dr}{dt} \), so to solve the problem we need to rewrite \( V \) solely as a function of \( h \). This is why need the relation \( 2r = h \).

\[ \begin{aligned} V &= \frac{\pi}{3} \pi r^2 h \\ V &= \frac{\pi}{3} \pi \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 h \end{aligned} \]\[ \begin{aligned} V &= \frac{\pi}{12} h^3 \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= \frac{\pi}{4} h^2 \frac{dh}{dt} \\ \frac{dh}{dt} &= \frac{4}{\pi h^2} \frac{dV}{dt} \\ \left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right]_{h=8} &= \frac{4}{\pi (8)^2} (15) \\ \left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right]_{h=8} &= \frac{60}{64\pi} \\ \left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right]_{h=8} &= \frac{15}{16\pi} \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, when the pile is 8cm tall, the height is increasing at a rate of \( \frac{15}{16\pi} \frac{\mathrm{cm}}{\mathrm{s}} \).
