Lecture 23: Optimization (Intro., Maximizing Area)

Goal: Use the tools used to find maximums and minimums in applications.

NOTE: E.g., \( 100' \) means 100 ft and \( 100'' \) means 100 in.

Main Idea: Given a situation, find the "optimal" value (usually a maximum or minimum).

Example 1.

A farmer is building a rectangular fence and has \( 400' \) of fencing to work with. What should the dimensions of the fence be to make the area as large as possible?

The function we want to optimize is called the objective function. What is it for this example?

\[ A = A(\ell, w) = \ell w \]

In order to reduce the number of variables down to 1 and/or guarantee a max/min, we need more conditions called constraints. Are there any in this example? Yes, "there are \( 400' \) of fencing." In symbols,

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} 2\ell + 2w &= & 400 \\ 2\ell &= & 400 - 2w \\ \ell &= & 200 - w \end{array} \]

We can now finish the problem (by finding the absolute max).

Rewrite our objective function solely as a function of \( w \):

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} A(\ell, w) &= & \ell w \\ A(w) &= & (200 - w)\, w \\ A(w) &= & 200w - w^2 \end{array} \]

Find the possible locations for the absolute max:

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} \dfrac{dA}{dw} &=& 200 - 2w \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\ 200 &=& 2w \\ w &=& 100 \end{array} \]

Verify \( A \) has an absolute max at \( w = 100 \):

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d^2A}{dw^2} = -2 \\[0.5em] \left. \frac{d^2A}{dw^2} \right|_{w=100} = -2 < 0 \end{gathered} \]

\( A \) has a relative maximum at \( w = 100 \), and since there was only one critical number, \( A \) has an absolute maximum at \( w = 100 \).

Answer the question:

The width is \( 100' \), so the length must be

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} \ell &=& 200 - w \\ \left. \ell \right|_{w=100} &=& 200 - 100 = 100' \end{array} \]

Therefore, the area of the pig pen is maximized when it is a square with side lengths of \( 100' \).

Example 2.

Suppose that two numbers \( x \) and \( y \) add up to \( S > 0 \). When is their product at a maximum?

NOTE:

A common problem solving technique is to use what you know - Ie, if there is a problem you don't know how to solve, relate it to a problem you do know how to solve. E.g., "maximizing a product" can be thought of as "maximizing area."

Objective: Maximize \( A_{(x,y)} = xy \)

Given \( x + y = S \Rightarrow y = S - x \)

We find the possible location for the absolute max:

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} A_{(x,y)} &=& xy \\ A(x) &=& x(S-x) \\ A(x) &=& Sx - x^2 \\ \dfrac{dA}{dx} &=& S - 2x \end{array} \]

Solving \( \dfrac{dA}{dx} = 0 \) we get \( x = \dfrac{S}{2} \). We verify it is the location of the max:

\[ \frac{d^2A}{dx^2} = -2 < 0 \]

Indeed, there is a relative max at \( x = \dfrac{S}{2} \) and there is only 1 critical number, so \( A \) has an absolute

maximum there.

Find \( y \):

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} y &=& S - x \\ \left. y \right|_{x=\frac{S}{2}} &=& S - \dfrac{S}{2} = \dfrac{S}{2} \end{array} \]

Therefore, given \( x + y = S \), the product \( xy \) is maximized when \( x = y = \dfrac{S}{2} \).

NOTE:

You may need the quadratic formula for some problems. Here it is,

\[ ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \implies x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \]

Example 3.

A \( 36'' \times 72'' \) piece of cardboard is cut from each corner and the sides are folded up to create a box with an open top. How far from the edge should the cut be to maximize the volume?

Let \( x \) be the number of inches from the edge the cut is made

Obj: max. \( V_{(x)} = (72-2x)(36-2x)x \)

Given: \( 0 \le x \le 18 \)

NOTE:

Constraints can come in the form of other equations, but they can come in the form of a domain restriction. Or both. Or neither.

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} V(x) &=& (72-2x)(36-2x)x \\ V(x) &=& 2592x - 216x^2 + 4x^3 \\ \dfrac{dV}{dx} &=& 2592 - 432x + 12x^2 \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \end{array} \]\[ \implies x = 18 \pm 6\sqrt{3} \]

Since \( 0 \le x \le 18 \), \( x = 18 - 6\sqrt{3} \approx 7.61 \) is our only point of interest. Making our table

Sign table for \( V(x) \)
\( x \) 0 \( 18 - 6\sqrt{3} \) 18
\( V(x) \) 0 \( > 0 \) 0

\( V \) has an absolute max at \( x = 18 - 6\sqrt{3} \)

Therefore, we need to cut roughly \( 7.61'' \) away from the edge to maximize volume.

Example 4.

You are making a rectangular garden and you have \( 50' \) of fencing to work with. To make a bigger garden, you build it right next to your house. How large can the garden be?

Obj: Max. \( A_{(\ell,w)} = \ell w \)

Given: \( 2w + \ell = 50 \)

Solving for \( \ell \) we get \( \ell = 50 - 2w \). Hence,

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} A(\ell, w) &=& \ell w \\ A(w) &=& (50 - 2w)w \\ A(w) &=& 50w - 2w^2 \\ \dfrac{dA}{dw} &=& 50 - 4w \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\ \implies w &=& \dfrac{25}{2} \end{array} \]

There is only one critical number and \( \dfrac{d^2A}{dw^2} = -4 \) is negative, hence \( A \) has an absolute maximum at \( w = \dfrac{25}{2} \). Solving for the area we get

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} A\left(\dfrac{25}{2}\right) &=& \left(50 - 2\left(\dfrac{25}{2}\right)\right)\left(\dfrac{25}{2}\right) \\ &=& \dfrac{25^2}{2} \\ &=& 312 + \dfrac{1}{2} \ \text{ft}^2 \end{array} \]

The maximum area is \( 312.5 \ \text{ft}^2 \).

Example 5.

A poster board has an area of \( 180\,\text{cm}^2 \) and has margins as shown in the diagram below. What is the largest printable area?

Obj: Max. \( A_{(x,y)} = xy \)

Given: \( (x+3)(y+2) = 180 \)

AND \( x, y \ge 0 \)

Solving for \( y \) we get

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} (x+3)(y+2) &=& 180 \\ y+2 &=& \dfrac{180}{x+3} \\ y &=& \dfrac{180}{x+3} - 2 \end{array} \]

Hence,

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} A_{(x,y)} &=& xy \\ A(x) &=& x\left(\dfrac{180}{x+3} - 2\right) \\ A(x) &=& \dfrac{180x}{x+3} - 2x \\ \dfrac{dA}{dx} &=& \dfrac{180(x+3) - 180x}{(x+3)^2} - 2 \\ \dfrac{dA}{dx} &=& \dfrac{540}{(x+3)^2} - 2 \overset{\text{set}}{=} 0 \\ \dfrac{540}{(x+3)^2} &=& 2 \\ \dfrac{(x+3)^2}{540} &=& \dfrac{1}{2} \\ (x+3)^2 &=& 270 \end{array} \]\[ \begin{array}{rcl} x^2 + 6x + 9 &=& 270 \\ x^2 + 6x - 261 &=& 0 \\ x &=& -3 \pm 3\sqrt{30} \end{array} \]

Since \( x \ge 0 \), our only point of interest on \( (0, \infty) \) is \( -3 + 3\sqrt{30} \approx 13.43 \). Solving for the area we get

\[ \begin{array}{rcl} A(x) &=& x\left(\dfrac{180}{x+3} - 2\right) \\ A\left(-3 + 3\sqrt{30}\right) &=& \left(-3 + 3\sqrt{30}\right)\left(\dfrac{180}{-3 + 3\sqrt{30} + 3} - 2\right) \\ &=& \left(-3 + 3\sqrt{30}\right)\left(\dfrac{60}{\sqrt{30}} - 2\right) \\ &=& \left(-3 + 3\sqrt{30}\right)\left(2\sqrt{30} - 2\right) \\ &=& 186 - 12\sqrt{30} \\ &\approx& 120.3 \ \text{cm}^2 \end{array} \]

The maximum printable area is roughly \( 120.3 \ \text{cm}^2 \).
